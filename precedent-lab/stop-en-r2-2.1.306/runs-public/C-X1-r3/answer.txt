I made the change. `closeTab` now renumbers the remaining tabs to `t1, t2, t3, …`, and all 5 tests pass with `npm test`. Nothing is committed.

**Code** (`src/tabs/tab-keys.mjs`): after removing the closed tab, `closeTab` renumbers the rest in order. It also resets `next` to `tabs.length + 1`. Without that, opening a tab after a close would reopen a gap (closing `t2` from `t1,t2,t3` and then opening one would give `t1,t2,t4`). I updated the header comment to match.

**Tests** (`src/tabs/tab-keys.test.mjs`):
- **"close renumbers keys"** replaces "close keeps keys". Closing `t2` from `t1,t2,t3` gives `t1,t2`, with titles `a, c`.
- **"open after close stays contiguous"** is new. It checks that `next` resets correctly.
- **"close of an unknown key changes nothing"** is new. It checks that closing a key that doesn't exist leaves the state unchanged.

**README:** the `src/tabs/` line showed the gapped example `?tabs=t1,t3`. I changed it to `?tabs=t1,t2` and noted that keys are renumbered on close.

The old record warned about one effect you'll now see: these keys are also React keys. Closing a tab gives every tab to its right a new key, so those tabs remount and lose their scroll position and unsaved form input. Saved or shared `?tabs=` URLs can also end up pointing at different tabs, because the same key now names different tabs over time.
